## What do you need to start a suit brand?

To start a suit brand you need a clear customer, a small first collection of 3 to 5 styles, a manufacturer that can produce under your label, and enough budget for samples, production, branding, photography and your first marketing. New labels more often struggle with stock and cash flow than with design. Keeping the first collection small is the best protection.

This guide is based on the questions new brands ask us most often when they plan their first private label order.

## Step 1: Decide who you are selling to

Pick one customer and one price level before you design anything. A suit for a 25-year-old buying online for a wedding is a different product from a suit for a 45-year-old lawyer buying in a boutique.

Answer these questions in writing:

- Who buys the suit, and for what occasion: work, weddings, events, everyday?
- What retail price will they pay?
- Where will they buy it: your website, marketplaces, your own shop, other retailers?
- Which country or countries will you sell in first?
- Why would they choose you over the suits already available to them?

Your answers decide fabric, fit and margin. If your retail price is fixed, the factory price and fabric have to fit inside it.

## Step 2: Plan a small first collection

Launch with 3 to 5 styles, built around colours and fits that sell every year. A focused collection is cheaper to produce, easier to photograph and easier for customers to understand.

A typical first collection might be:

1. A navy 2-piece suit, slim or modern fit, notch lapel.
2. A charcoal or grey 2-piece suit in the same fit.
3. A 3-piece suit with waistcoat, for weddings and events.
4. Optional: a black tuxedo or a summer linen suit, depending on your customer.

Navy and grey sell in almost every market. Keep fashion colours and statement pieces for your second collection, when you have sales data. You can browse [2-piece suits](/mens-suit-manufacturing/2-piece-suits) and other categories to see base models we can adapt for you.

## Step 3: Plan quantities around the MOQ

Our minimum for made-to-order under your brand is 100 units per style, so a 3-style launch means about 300 suits. The 100 units are spread across your size run, so you might have 15 to 22 pieces in your best sizes and fewer at the ends.

Before you commit, check that you can sell that quantity within a season or two. If you are not sure, there are two lower-risk routes:

- Start with fewer styles at 100 units each, and add styles as they sell.
- Test the market first with ready-stock lots of our own models, then move to your own label once you know which styles work.

Our [stock lots vs made-to-order guide](/guides/stock-lots-vs-made-to-order) compares both routes.

## Step 4: List your budget items

Your budget is more than the factory price per suit. New brands often forget the costs around production. Here is what to plan for:

| Budget item | What it covers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Samples | Prototypes and fit samples | Plan for at least one round of changes |
| Production | Suits at the quoted unit price | Quoted per model, fabric and quantity |
| Branding | Neck labels, care labels, printed lining, buttons, hangers, garment bags, boxes | Some items have their own minimums at the supplier |
| Shipping | Air or sea freight, door to door or to your forwarder | Quoted per destination |
| Import costs | Customs duty, VAT or sales tax, broker fees | Depends on your country, check with a customs broker |
| Photography | Product and model photos | Essential for online sales |
| Website and sales channels | Store build, marketplace fees, payment fees | Ongoing costs |
| Marketing | Ads, content, influencers, launch event | Often the biggest ongoing cost |
| Working capital | Cash to cover reorders and slow months | Keep a reserve |

We do not publish prices, because they depend on model, fabric, quantity and branding. Ask for a quote with your target retail price and we will suggest options that fit.

## Step 5: Get samples right

Approve a sample before any bulk production, and check it on real people in your main sizes. The sample is where you fix fit, fabric and details. Changing them after production starts is expensive.

When you review a sample:

- Try it on two or three people of different builds, not only a mannequin.
- Check the fit in shoulders, chest, waist and trouser rise against your target customer.
- Check the fabric in daylight and after a day of wear.
- Confirm lining, buttons and labels look the way you want.
- Write your changes down clearly and send photos.

We do pattern making and sampling in-house, with CAD pattern design and 3D prototyping, which helps when you make changes between rounds.

## Step 6: Design your labels and packaging

Your branding should be ready before production starts. Private label means the suit carries your brand throughout: woven neck label, size and care labels, lining, buttons and packaging.

- **Neck label:** woven with your logo. Send a vector file.
- **Care and composition label:** must meet the rules of your market, for example fibre content wording in the EU or UK.
- **Lining:** plain in a colour you choose, or printed with your pattern.
- **Buttons:** standard, or engraved with your logo.
- **Packaging:** branded hangers, garment bags, boxes.

Our [private label suits](/private-label-suits) page lists the options.

## Step 7: Plan photography and sales

Book photography for when your goods arrive, and plan your launch around it. Good photos sell suits online more than any product description.

Decide early how you will sell:

| Channel | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Direct to consumer (your website, social media) | Higher margin, you own the customer | You pay for traffic and handle returns |
| Marketplaces | Built-in traffic | Fees, price competition |
| Wholesale to other shops | Larger orders, less marketing per unit | Lower margin per suit, retailers expect terms |
| Your own shop or showroom | Customers can try on, fewer returns | Rent and staff costs |

Many new labels combine online sales with a few stockists, then grow whichever channel works.

## Step 8: Work with a Turkish manufacturer

Send a clear brief, approve a sample, confirm everything in writing, and plan your timeline backwards from your launch date. Istanbul is close to Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Central Asia, and Turkish factories are used to working with small and growing brands as well as large ones.

When you contact a manufacturer, include:

1. Photos or sketches of the styles, or our model codes to adapt.
2. Fabric preference and target price.
3. Fit and size run.
4. Quantities per style and colour.
5. Branding items you need.
6. Destination country.

## What is a realistic timeline?

Plan for about three to five months from first contact to launch. Most of that time goes on design decisions, sampling and branding, not production.

| Stage | Typical time |
|---|---|
| Positioning and collection plan | 2-4 weeks |
| Tech details, fabric selection, quote | 1-3 weeks |
| Samples and changes | 2-6 weeks, depending on rounds of changes |
| Labels and packaging | Run alongside sampling |
| Production | Typically 12-15 days, depending on quantity, model and fabric |
| Shipping and customs | Depends on destination and method |
| Photography and launch | 2-4 weeks |

Your own speed of decision is the biggest variable. Brands that approve samples quickly launch faster.

## FAQ

### How many suits do I need to start a brand?

With our made-to-order minimum of 100 units per style, a three-style launch is about 300 suits. You can start with fewer styles, or test with stock lots before ordering under your label.

### Can I start with one style?

Yes. One style at 100 units is possible. Many new brands start with a navy suit and add styles once they see what sells.

### Do I need my own designs?

No. You can adapt one of our existing models with your fabric, lining, buttons and labels. Brands with their own technical specs can use our [OEM service](/oem-suit-manufacturing).

### Can I order samples before production?

Yes. We make samples in-house so you can approve fit, fabric and details before bulk production. Ask us about sample timing and cost for your styles.

### Do you help with shipping and customs?

We ship worldwide by air or sea, door to door or to your forwarder, and quote shipping per destination. For import duties and taxes in your country, we recommend working with a local customs broker.
