## The short answer

China and Bangladesh are usually cheaper per unit at very large volumes. Italy is the reference for premium tailoring at a premium price. Turkey sits in between: good tailoring, short lead times, flexible MOQs and fast delivery to Europe, Africa, the Middle East and Central Asia.

The right country depends on your volume, your market and how quickly you need to restock. Below is how we see it, including the cases where Turkey is not the best choice.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit cost | Mid | Low to mid | Lowest at high volume | High |

Treat this as a general picture. Individual factories in every country can be better or worse than the average.

## Cost: who is cheapest?

At very large volumes, Bangladesh, Vietnam and China usually win on unit price. Labour costs are lower and the factories are built for long runs of the same style.

Turkey is not the lowest-cost country, and buyers should not expect it to be. Where Turkey competes is total landed cost on mid-size orders. When you add freight, the cost of holding stock during a long sea voyage, and the risk of missing a season, the gap often narrows. For buyers in Europe, North Africa and the Middle East, it can close entirely.

Italy is the premium option. You pay for heritage mills, canvas construction and the "Made in Italy" label.

## MOQ flexibility

Turkey is generally more flexible on minimums than the large Asian export factories. Many Turkish suit makers accept made-to-order runs of around 100 pieces per style, and some also sell ready stock in small size-run lots.

Large factories in China, Bangladesh and Vietnam are set up for volume. Smaller Chinese workshops will accept low quantities, but quality and consistency vary more. Italian makers often accept small runs, but at a much higher unit price.

## Lead time and speed to market

This is Turkey's strongest point. Fabric, trims and labour are close together, and goods reach Europe by truck in days. A buyer can reorder a selling model mid-season instead of guessing a full season's volume months ahead.

From Asia, sea freight alone to Europe or West Africa takes several weeks. Air freight closes the gap but adds cost per suit.

At our factory, production typically takes 12-15 days after fabric and details are confirmed, depending on quantity and model.

## Tailoring quality

A suit jacket is one of the most demanding garments in menswear: shoulder shaping, chest fusing or canvas, collar setting and pressing all show in the final look.

- **Turkey** has a long tradition of men's tailoring and supplies many European high-street and mid-market brands. Most factories do fused and half-canvas construction well.
- **China** covers the full range. The best Chinese tailoring factories are very good; the cheapest are not. Choosing the right supplier matters a lot.
- **Bangladesh and Vietnam** are strongest in volume programs, often fused construction in standard fits.
- **Italy** leads on hand-finished and full-canvas suits.

Whichever country you choose, judge the sample, not the flag.

## Communication and visits

Turkish factories usually work in English and Turkish, sit one to two hours ahead of most of Europe, and are a short flight from most target markets. Problems can be solved on a same-day call or a quick visit.

With Asian suppliers, the time difference and distance make visits rarer, so buyers rely more on agents and third-party inspectors. That works, but it adds a layer.

## When Turkey is not the best choice

We would rather be honest than lose your trust:

- If you buy tens of thousands of identical basic suits a season and price is the only factor, a large Asian factory will likely beat us.
- If you sell to a luxury customer who wants full canvas and an Italian label, Italy is the obvious choice.

Turkey makes most sense for buyers who want good tailoring, reasonable minimums, quick reorders and short routes to their market.

## Which country fits which buyer

A simple way to decide:

| Buyer profile | Usually the best fit |
|---|---|
| Mass-market chain, very large runs of basic suits | China, Bangladesh or Vietnam |
| Mid-size retailer or wholesaler, 100 to a few thousand units per style | Turkey |
| Online brand that reorders bestsellers during the season | Turkey |
| Wedding and formalwear shops needing tuxedos and ceremony styles quickly | Turkey |
| Luxury brand selling full-canvas tailoring | Italy |

Many buyers use more than one country. The question is which part of your range needs low cost, which needs speed and which needs a premium label.

## Hidden costs to include

When comparing countries, include more than the unit price: freight, duties for your market, the cost of capital tied up in stock during transit, sample shipping, inspection and the cost of unsold stock if you have to order too early. These often decide the real winner.

## FAQ

### Is it cheaper to make suits in Turkey or China?

At large volumes, China is usually cheaper per unit. On mid-size orders shipped to Europe, Africa or the Middle East, the landed cost difference is often smaller once freight and time are included.

### Are Turkish suits good quality?

Turkey has a strong men's tailoring industry and supplies many European brands. Quality varies by factory, so always approve a sample before ordering.

### Why do European buyers choose Turkey for suits?

Short lead times, truck delivery in days, flexible minimums and the EU-Turkey Customs Union for industrial goods. Confirm the duty position with your customs broker.

### Can I split production between Turkey and Asia?

Yes, many brands do. A common setup is core basics in Asia and fashion colours, ceremony styles and fast reorders in Turkey.
