## The short answer

To import suits from Turkey to Africa, you choose your models, agree a quote with the factory, pay in USD or EUR, and ship by air or sea, either through the factory or your own cargo agent in Istanbul. The goods travel with a commercial invoice, packing list and, where needed, a certificate of origin. Your customs broker at home handles clearance and tells you the duties.

Istanbul is one of the most practical sourcing cities for African menswear buyers. There are frequent flights to most African capitals, many cargo agents who consolidate shipments for African traders, and factories used to colourful ceremony styles as well as classic business suits.

## What sells in African markets

Every market is different, and your own customers are the best guide. In general terms, these are the requests we hear most from African buyers:

| Region | Commonly requested styles |
|---|---|
| West Africa (Nigeria, Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, Cameroon) | Bold colours, 3-piece suits, slim fits, shiny and jacquard tuxedos for weddings and events |
| Central Africa (DR Congo) | Strong colours, statement tuxedos, double-breasted, sharp tailoring |
| East Africa (Kenya) | Business suits in navy and grey, 3-piece wedding suits |
| Southern Africa (South Africa) | Business suits, wedding and matric-dance styles, slim and modern fits |
| North Africa (Algeria, Morocco, Egypt, Libya, Tunisia) | Classic business suits, wedding suits for grooms and families, dark colours, 3-piece ceremony styles |

Lighter fabrics and lighter construction sell better in hot climates. Ask for summer-weight cloth, half lining or linen-blend options for warm markets. ## How to buy: stock lots or made to order

There are two ways to buy from us:

- **Ready stock in lots.** Many models are sold as size-run lots, for example one model in EU sizes 46-48-50-52-54-56, six suits per lot. Good for testing a market or mixing many models in one shipment.
- **Made to order.** From 100 units per style, in your choice of fabric, colour and fit, with your own labels if you want them.

We work with businesses only. The smallest order is one lot; we do not sell single suits.

## Air or sea freight

Air is faster and suits smaller and urgent orders. Sea is cheaper per suit for large volumes but takes longer.

| | Air freight | Sea freight |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Fastest | Slower, weeks rather than days |
| Cost per suit | Higher | Lower at volume |
| Best for | First orders, wedding season top-ups, mixed lots | Large repeat orders, full containers |
| Packing | Folded in cartons to save volume | Folded or hanging, cartons or container |

Many buyers in West and Central Africa also use consolidated cargo services, where several traders share space on the same flight or container.

We quote shipping per destination, either door-to-door or delivered to your forwarder. See our [shipping page](/shipping).

## Working with a cargo agent in Istanbul

Many African importers already have a trusted cargo agent in Istanbul, often in the Laleli or airport areas. That works well with us:

1. You give us your agent's name, address and phone.
2. We pack and label the cartons with your marks.
3. Your agent collects or we deliver to their warehouse.
4. We send the commercial invoice and packing list to you and the agent.

Tips:

- Agree carton marks in advance so your goods are easy to separate in a consolidated shipment.
- Ask your agent whether they need the invoice in a specific format.
- Check what the agent includes: some quote "door-to-door including clearance", others only airport to airport.

## Documents you will need

The standard export documents for suits are:

- **Commercial invoice:** buyer, seller, description, HS code, quantity, unit price, total value, delivery term.
- **Packing list:** cartons, pieces per carton, weights and dimensions.
- **Certificate of origin:** issued through the Turkish chamber of commerce when required by your country or your broker.
- **Air waybill or bill of lading:** issued by the carrier or forwarder.

Some countries ask for additional documents, such as import permits, pre-shipment inspection or electronic cargo tracking notes. Requirements change, so ask your customs broker before shipping. Duty and tax rates on clothing vary widely across Africa; please confirm them with your broker, as we cannot quote your import duties.

## Paying for your order

Most buyers pay by international bank transfer in USD or EUR. A common practice in garment trade is a deposit when you confirm the order and the balance before shipment. All terms are written on the proforma invoice.

Practical advice:

- Pay only to the company account named on the proforma invoice.
- If bank details ever change by email or message, confirm by phone or video call before you pay.
- Keep the bank transfer receipt and send it to the factory so the payment can be matched quickly.

## Timing for wedding and festive seasons

Wedding and festive seasons drive much of the suit demand in many African markets. Plan backwards: production is typically 12-15 days at our factory once the order is confirmed, then add shipping and clearance time. Sea freight needs much earlier ordering than air.

## Checklist before you order

- Models, colours and quantities chosen
- Size range checked against your customers
- Air or sea decided
- Cargo agent or door-to-door delivery decided
- Duties and documents confirmed with your customs broker
- Payment terms agreed on the proforma invoice

## Mixing models in one shipment

A single shipment does not have to be one model. Most African buyers we work with mix several ready-stock lots, for example a few lots of black and navy business suits, some 3-piece wedding suits and a couple of lots of colourful tuxedos. This spreads the risk and lets you see which models sell before you reorder in volume.

When you mix models, keep the order sheet simple: one line per model and colour, the number of lots, and the size run in each lot. We pack each model in clearly marked cartons, so your shop staff can unpack and price quickly.

If you want your own brand on the suits, private label production starts from 100 units per style. Many importers start with stock lots and move a best-selling model to private label once they know it sells.

## Avoiding common problems

- **Unclear carton marks.** In consolidated cargo, unmarked cartons get mixed with other traders' goods. Agree marks before packing.
- **Undervalued invoices.** Ask for an invoice that reflects the real value. Problems at customs cost more than the duty saved.
- **Late orders for the season.** Order early enough for sea freight, or budget for air.
- **Wrong sizes.** Check your customers' builds before choosing the size run.
